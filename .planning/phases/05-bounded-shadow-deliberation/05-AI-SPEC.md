# AI-SPEC — Phase 5: Bounded Shadow Deliberation

> AI design contract generated by `$gsd-ai-integration-phase`. Consumed by
> `gsd-planner` and `gsd-eval-auditor`.

## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Hybrid

**Description:** Live Galaxy invokes a provider-neutral model boundary to
propose bounded Shadow initiatives, plans, institutional responses, and the
typed ZYA–ARG diplomatic posture. The Rust kernel remains authoritative: it
validates every proposal and persists only admitted state. Before `1.0.0`,
real-model runs use a developer-controlled subscription harness; deterministic
fakes cover contracts and replay but cannot prove strategic quality.

**Critical Failure Modes:**

1. A model proposal bypasses schema, semantic, information, safety, budget, or
   current-state validation.
2. Hidden or stale information influences a proposal or cache hit.
3. Retry, recovery, or replay duplicates an initiative or changes an admitted
   result for identical authoritative inputs.
4. Deliberation exceeds the two-cycle dialogue cap or any configured resource
   bound.
5. Provider failure corrupts accepted state or causes speculative X4 mutation.

## 1b. Domain Context

**Industry Vertical:** Grand-strategy simulation and X4 mod development.

**User Population:** The Live Galaxy owner and experienced X4/mod-test
practitioners evaluating an internal 0.1 prototype; this is not a public-facing
or playable release.

**Stakes Level:** High for prototype integrity. A poor Shadow result must not
alter X4, but an admitted incoherent or irreproducible result would invalidate
the evidence used to decide whether the director is fit to progress.

**Output Consequence:** A provider candidate that passes deterministic
admission becomes persistent, typed Shadow plan, initiative, or ZYA–ARG
posture evidence. It can influence later reporting and evaluation but cannot
execute an X4 command or mutate game state in milestone 0.1.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

**Dimension: Faction doctrine expressed under pressure**

**Good (domain expert would accept):** Given the same XEN pressure and a
faction-visible packet, ZYA and ARG choose materially different priorities,
trade-offs, or horizons that follow their documented doctrine and current
constraints; the difference is visible in typed goals and supporting facts,
not just faction labels or prose style.

**Bad (domain expert would flag):** Both factions emit the same generic
“secure borders / strengthen economy” plan, or a proposal contradicts its
faction's doctrine without a recorded, visible trigger.

**Stakes:** High

**Source:** Documented Live Galaxy MIND-05/06 and Phase 5 goal; strategic-game
practice treats personality/strategic orientation as upstream of differentiated
objectives (analogy: Rome Remastered AI diplomacy guide).

**Dimension: Causal strategic continuity**

**Good (domain expert would accept):** The proposal references the current
initiative and frozen visible evidence, either advances its stated horizon or
records a specific event and explicit preemption/revision disposition that
explains the change. Replaying the same authoritative inputs preserves the
same admitted history.

**Bad (domain expert would flag):** A faction reverses, replaces, or restarts
an initiative without a visible trigger, predecessor state, or retained reason;
the same input produces unexplained replacement history.

**Stakes:** Critical

**Source:** Documented Phase 5 success criteria, INST-05, and exact-replay
contract; agent-based simulation validation relies on explicit invariants
(VOMAS research, adapted as an evaluation method rather than X4 fact).

**Dimension: Institution and Executive role consistency**

**Good (domain expert would accept):** Defense/military, economy/logistics,
and territorial/infrastructure institutions propose work within their capability
contract; the Executive makes one explicit typed disposition, preserves a
single owner, and opens dialogue only for a material objection, mandate,
revision, or preemption.

**Bad (domain expert would flag):** An institution silently acts outside its
role, multiple active initiatives compete for its ownership, or routine
agreement creates free-form council dialogue instead of a terminal disposition.

**Stakes:** Critical

**Source:** Documented Phase 5 scope, INST-04 through INST-07, and project
institution contract; modding practice identifies cross-subsystem local
optimization as a visible strategic-coherence failure (Anbeeld, analogy).

**Dimension: Faction knowledge discipline**

**Good (domain expert would accept):** Every cited fact is present in the
frozen faction-visible packet, current enough for the request, and sufficient
to support the stated goal, trade-off, and posture. Unknown, stale, or
cross-faction facts result in rejection or a bounded degraded outcome.

**Bad (domain expert would flag):** A plan names hidden enemy capacity,
unobserved intent, another faction's private view, or stale facts as if they
were known; a cache hit carries those facts forward without revalidation.

**Stakes:** Critical

**Source:** Documented project information-discipline constraint and
MODEL-02/04; this is a project trust-boundary rule, not an inference about X4.

**Dimension: Bounded, non-repetitive strategic conduct**

**Good (domain expert would accept):** Work is deduplicated per faction,
dialogue ends within two cycles, and a plan changes only when a relevant visible
event, mandate, or lifecycle transition warrants it. Repeated ticks may retain
a justified initiative instead of inventing superficial variation.

**Bad (domain expert would flag):** The director repeatedly rephrases the same
initiative, churns preemptions to look active, or loops through Executive and
institution responses until a preferred answer appears.

**Stakes:** High

**Source:** Documented D-06 through D-08, MIND-07, INST-06/07, and MODEL-06;
strategic-simulation benchmark practice measures robustness as well as outcome
(GENSTRAT, analogy).

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **Faction flattening:** ZYA and ARG use interchangeable plans with cosmetic
   names, breaking the core promise of distinct faction minds.
2. **Strategic amnesia or thrash:** a current initiative is replaced, restarted,
   or contradicted without a visible event, recorded predecessor, or terminal
   Executive decision.
3. **Institutional silo conflict:** locally plausible defense, economy, and
   territorial proposals compete without an Executive-owned trade-off or a
   stable initiative owner.
4. **Omniscient or stale simulation:** a faction plan relies on information it
   could not observe, or an exact-cache result is mistaken for current strategic
   evidence.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** Direct Rust provider trait with a Serde/JSON Schema
proposal boundary; no agent framework.

**Version:** Workspace Rust toolchain and reviewed workspace dependency
versions; no AI framework package.

**Rationale:** Phase 5 is a bounded provider adapter around existing typed
strategic packets, persistent faction minds, deterministic admission, and exact
replay identity. A second orchestration or persistence framework would duplicate
kernel-owned behavior and weaken the trust boundary.

**Alternatives Considered:**

## 3. Framework Quick Reference

**Framework:** No agent framework. Phase 5 uses a direct, provider-neutral Rust
port with Serde JSON envelopes and a versioned JSON Schema contract. This is an
intentional boundary, not a temporary omission: LangGraph remains a rejected
future alternative because it would introduce a second orchestration and state
plane beside the bounded Rust state machine.

**Workspace setup:** No new installation or dependency is authorized by this
contract. The workspace already pins `serde` with `derive` and `serde_json` in
`mind-persistence`; promote shared contract types only after Phase 5 planning
confirms their owning crate. JSON Schema generation and runtime-validation
crates are **provisional** until that plan reviews their need, supported draft,
MSRV, licensing, and error behavior. Serde deserialization alone is necessary
but not sufficient for admission.

**Entry pattern:** Build the frozen, faction-visible packet synchronously in
the deterministic kernel. The orchestration shell computes the exact cache key,
calls `ShadowProvider::propose`, then passes raw bytes through schema parsing
and deterministic admission. The provider never receives an X4 command port or
a persistence handle; an admitted result is still Shadow-only.

| Abstraction | Responsibility in Phase 5 | Boundary rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ShadowProvider` | Converts a typed request into one bounded harness or fake invocation and returns raw candidate bytes plus provider metadata. | Provider-specific protocol stays in its adapter. |
| `DeliberationRequest` | Versioned, faction-scoped envelope containing the frozen packet, policy version, prompt package hash, and invocation budget. | Construct only after visibility filtering and canonical ordering. |
| `ShadowProposal` | Serde-deserializable candidate containing plan, initiative disposition, posture, evidence references, and trade-offs. | Untrusted until every validator accepts it. |
| `ProposalSchema` and `AdmissionValidator` | Respectively enforce the published JSON Schema and deterministic schema/semantic/information/safety/budget/current-state checks. | Neither coerces unknown fields, missing facts, or stale IDs into a valid candidate. |
| `ExactCacheKey` | Canonical identity for a completed provider result or admitted outcome. | Includes faction, snapshot, policy, prompt package, schema, provider, model, and generation settings. |

**Pitfalls specific to this boundary:**

1. Do not deserialize into `serde_json::Value` and inspect fields ad hoc: absent
   or misspelled fields can appear as `Null`, bypassing compiler guidance.
   Deserialize bytes directly into a `#[serde(deny_unknown_fields)]` envelope,
   then perform semantic admission.
2. Do not make a provider retry by regenerating request IDs, using wall-clock
   state, or rebuilding a slightly different prompt. Retries must reuse the
   canonical request and cache identity or end in a recorded degraded result.
3. Do not place async provider calls, locks, or provider response types in the
   deterministic kernel. This breaks replay and risks holding state across an
   await boundary.
4. Do not treat JSON Schema compliance as strategic validity. A syntactically
   valid proposal may cite hidden, stale, unsupported, over-budget, or
   superseded facts and must still be rejected without persistence side effects.
5. Do not treat a ChatGPT subscription as a runtime API. Pre-alpha real-model
   runs are developer-controlled harness evidence only; public provider API and
   local-model adapters are out of scope.

**Recommended module ownership (split before the 200-line source limit):**

```text
crates/
  mind-domain/src/deliberation/{request,proposal,schema,admission}.rs
  mind-orchestration/src/{provider_port,cache,runner,degraded}.rs
  provider-harness/src/{subscription_adapter,fake,fixtures}.rs
```

The paths are a Phase 5 ownership sketch, not permission to create crates or
dependencies before the plan establishes them.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model and parameters:** Provider/model selection is an empirical Phase 5
harness decision, not a product commitment. Every invocation must record the
provider ID, model ID, model revision when exposed, schema version, prompt
package hash, temperature/top-p (or explicit equivalents), seed if supported,
and explicit maximum output tokens. The initial operational values are
unmeasured: phase research must set them, along with request-byte, context,
wall-time, retry, queue-depth, per-faction call, and dialogue-cycle limits.
There is no unbounded default and no public runtime API configuration.

**Core provider/admission pattern:**

```rust
use serde::{Deserialize, Serialize};

#[derive(Debug, Deserialize, Serialize)]
#[serde(deny_unknown_fields)]
pub struct ShadowProposal {
    pub schema_version: String,
    pub plan: ProposedPlan,
    pub initiative: InitiativeDisposition,
    pub diplomatic_posture: DiplomaticPosture,
    pub supporting_fact_ids: Vec<FactId>,
    pub expected_trade_offs: Vec<TradeOff>,
}

pub trait ShadowProvider: Send + Sync {
    async fn propose(
        &self,
        request: DeliberationRequest,
    ) -> Result<ProviderResponse, ProviderFailure>;
}

pub async fn deliberate(
    provider: &dyn ShadowProvider,
    request: DeliberationRequest,
    cache: &dyn ExactCache,
    validator: &AdmissionValidator,
) -> Result<Admission, DeliberationFailure> {
    let key = ExactCacheKey::from_request(&request); // Canonical, versioned.
    if let Some(cached) = cache.get(&key)? {
        return validator.revalidate_cached(cached, &request);
    }

    let response = provider.propose(request.clone()).await?; // I/O is outside the kernel.
    let proposal: ShadowProposal = serde_json::from_slice(&response.candidate_bytes)
        .map_err(DeliberationFailure::MalformedProviderOutput)?;
    validator.validate_and_admit(proposal, request, response.metadata, key)
}
```

`ProposedPlan`, `InitiativeDisposition`, `DiplomaticPosture`, `FactId`,
`TradeOff`, cache interfaces, and error types are typed domain values to be
supplied by the owning Phase 4/5 crates. This snippet
illustrates the required seam, not an authorization to replace those types with
strings or introduce an async runtime.

**Prompt and context construction:** The provider adapter receives one
canonical prompt package: immutable system policy and output contract; the
faction doctrine and role; one frozen faction-visible strategic packet; current
typed mind/initiative state; and allowed primitives. Never concatenate raw X4 input, another faction's
view, provider output, or hidden reasoning. Stable facts are compacted into the
Phase 4 typed-plus-narrative capsule; newly relevant facts remain explicit
typed entries. Deterministically sort every list, carry omitted-section and
freshness metadata, and reject a request whose measured packet exceeds its
model-relative context budget rather than silently truncating it.

**Tool use:** None in Phase 5. A provider cannot call X4, persistence, shell,
network, or arbitrary tools. The only permissible response is the bounded JSON
candidate envelope. The future game-command port remains outside this phase and
behind deterministic admission.

**State, cache, and recovery:** Persist the replay tuple atomically with an
accepted Shadow plan: frozen snapshot ID and hash, ordered input-event IDs,
state version, faction and policy versions, compaction result, prompt-package
hash, schema/provider/model/generation identities, request budget, candidate
hash, validator version, validation outcome, and admitted IDs. Phase 6 may add
an idempotent report-outbox intent to that transaction. The exact cache key is
`(faction, snapshot_hash, policy_version, prompt_package_hash, schema_version,
provider_id, model_id, generation_settings)`. Cache data is non-authoritative:
a hit must be revalidated against the current snapshot/state before admission.

**Bounded execution and degraded mode:** Limit one deduplicated outstanding
request per faction, one candidate response per request, a plan-defined number
of same-identity transient retries, and at most two complete
Executive–institution dialogue cycles. On timeout, outage, invalid output, or
exhausted budget: persist a bounded failure classification and correlation ID;
admit nothing; preserve accepted state; pause new strategic decisions; and
reconcile fresh observations before replanning after recovery. Never partially
replace an initiative, advance a cursor, or mutate X4.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

### 4b.1 Structured outputs with Rust Serde and JSON Schema

The output schema is a Rust type plus a versioned JSON Schema artifact. Keep
`deny_unknown_fields` at the external response boundary and use named typed
identifiers and enums in nested domain values. Generate or maintain the schema
only through the reviewed Phase 5 mechanism; validate the raw JSON against it,
then deserialize with Serde, then run deterministic admission. These are three
separate gates.

```rust
#[derive(Debug, Deserialize, Serialize)]
#[serde(deny_unknown_fields)]
pub struct InitiativeDisposition {
    pub action: InitiativeAction,
    pub initiative_id: Option<InitiativeId>,
    pub replacement_reason: Option<ReplacementReason>,
    pub horizon: PlanningHorizon,
}

#[derive(Debug, Deserialize, Serialize)]
#[serde(rename_all = "snake_case")]
pub enum InitiativeAction {
    Originate,
    Assign,
    Approve,
    Revise,
    Preempt,
    Reject,
    Terminate,
}
```

On a schema or deserialization failure, record the request/cache identity,
provider/model fingerprint, schema version, bounded error class, response-byte
count, and candidate hash — never raw prompts, credentials, or hidden
reasoning. Permit at most one corrective retry using the identical frozen input
and an explicit “return one valid JSON candidate” repair instruction; then
surface a degraded failure. Semantic, information, safety, budget, or
current-state failures are not repaired by asking the provider to explain: they
are rejected deterministically and recorded for evaluation.

### 4b.2 Async-first boundary

`ShadowProvider::propose` is `async` because a harness/future adapter may wait
for external work. Keep the packet builder, exact-key construction, validators,
cache-key serializer, and state transitions synchronous and pure. The common
mistake is holding a persistence lock or borrowing mutable authoritative state
across `.await`; snapshot/copy the immutable request, release the lock, await
the provider, then enter one atomic validation/persistence step. Await the
single completed response for structured-output validation. Streaming is not a
Phase 5 transport: partial tokens are neither schema-valid candidates nor
player UX, and must not influence admission.

### 4b.3 Prompt engineering discipline

Keep static system policy/output constraints separate from the dynamic user-like
deliberation packet. The static package states the schema version, allowed
primitive vocabulary, no-tool/no-X4-mutation rule, and fact-ID citation rule.
The dynamic package contains only faction-visible,
fresh-enough packet facts plus typed current state. Do not use inline
few-shots by default: they consume scarce context and can blur faction doctrine.
If evaluation later proves examples help, retrieve versioned examples by exact
role/schema/policy compatibility and include their hashes in the prompt package
identity. Always set explicit maximum output tokens and request byte/context
caps; production-like harness runs never use provider defaults as a bound.

### 4b.4 Context-window management

Use the Phase 4 capsule as a compact, non-authoritative narrative aid while
preserving typed current facts as authority. Before invocation, build a
deterministic budget ledger: fixed policy/schema overhead, doctrine, required
current facts, active-initiative causal records, and optional older capsule
segments. Retain required current facts first; summarize or omit optional
history by stable priority and record every omission and compaction version.
If the minimum legal packet does not fit, do not truncate an allowed primitive,
visibility marker, freshness condition, or active-initiative predecessor;
degrade and wait for a smaller valid packet or a later schedule.

### 4b.5 Cost and latency budget

Before setting thresholds, benchmark the chosen developer-controlled harness on
representative ZYA/ARG packets and record input/output token estimates, elapsed
time, failure rate, cache-hit rate, and subscription-harness limitations. The
per-cycle budget is `max_provider_calls × (measured input + measured output
cost)` with separate limits for the normal fast path and exceptional two-cycle
dialogue; Phase 5 must replace this formula with measured numbers, not guessed
prices. Cache only exact identities, never semantic-near matches. Use the
deterministic kernel for routing, validity, deduplication, summarisation, and
ordinary degraded records; reserve real-model calls for the strategic candidate
that needs them. Any future cheap-model subtask remains provider-neutral and
requires a separate benchmark, schema, cache identity, and admission evidence.

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

Phase 5 has two deliberately separate evidence tracks. Deterministic Rust
tests prove trust-boundary, replay, and degradation contracts. They do **not**
prove that a strategy is good. Subscription-backed runs measure strategic
quality, but are benchmark evidence only until Phase 8 records a baseline and
sets acceptance thresholds. Disposable X4 and human evidence remains owned by
the later Phase 7/8 validation path; it is neither a Phase 5 blocker nor proof
from a fake adapter.

### Tooling and Gates

No evaluation platform, provider SDK, prompt framework, or telemetry dependency
is authorized. The existing Rust workspace, recorded typed fixtures,
deterministic fake provider, `cargo test`, and structured evidence manifests
are the tooling default. The repository scan found no Langfuse, LangSmith,
Phoenix, Braintrust, Promptfoo, or RAGAS integration; adding one would be
premature for this internal, provider-neutral prototype.

The implementation plan must expose the corpus as a focused Rust integration
test target in `mind-domain` (for example,
`crates/mind-domain/tests/shadow_deliberation_evals.rs`). CI then runs:

```text
cargo test -p mind-domain --test shadow_deliberation_evals --locked
cargo test --workspace --locked
```

The focused command is release-blocking for Phase 5: every CI case must pass,
the manifest must resolve every fixture hash, and failures must leave the
asserted authoritative state unchanged. Run `cargo fmt --check` and the
repository's strict Clippy command alongside the existing workspace gate once
the Phase 5 code exists. Mutation testing follows the Rust-test skill: measure
`cargo-mutants` only after representative pure admission logic exists; no
unmeasured mutation percentage is a gate.

Subscription-backed benchmark runs use the same versioned corpus plus complete
multi-cycle trajectories. They are invoked manually through the
developer-controlled harness, never normal CI and never a public runtime API.
Each run records corpus version/hash, harness revision, provider/model/revision,
generation settings, prompt/schema/policy hashes, start/end time, usage/cost
when exposed, cache state, and per-case outputs. A strategic-simulation expert
and the owner label doctrine divergence, continuity, and institution coherence
blind to model/provider where practical. Before a future LLM judge can inform a
decision, it must first be calibrated against those human labels with a
predeclared agreement target; no such judge is in scope now. Phase 8 alone sets
baseline-derived reliability and strategic-quality release thresholds.

Human X4 evidence is explicitly later: Phase 7 supplies disposable
normal-speed/SETA/reconnect/soak observations and labels them `observed in X4`;
Phase 8 combines that evidence with benchmarks. Phase 5 reports only
`verified locally` deterministic contracts and `benchmark evidence`, never a
playability or game-behavior claim.

## 6. Guardrails

| Guardrail | Trigger and action | Required recorded evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frozen visibility and freshness | Packet contains inaccessible, unknown, unsupported, or stale required evidence; reject before provider invocation when detectable, otherwise reject candidate. | Snapshot/hash, faction, policy, fact IDs/quality classes, rejection code; never raw private prompt. |
| Schema and semantic admission | Bytes fail size/schema/Serde checks, use unknown fields, invalid primitive, invalid lifecycle transition, or unsupported posture. Reject atomically. | Request/cache identity, schema/validator versions, candidate hash/size, bounded rejection class. |
| Safety and no-mutation boundary | Candidate requests a forbidden operation, an X4 mutation, or a capability outside the finite Shadow vocabulary. Reject; do not create report or command intent. | Candidate hash, policy version, denied capability class, zero-side-effect assertion. |
| Current-state, cache, and idempotency | Cached/result state no longer matches the frozen tuple, existing owner/initiative conflicts, or a duplicate admission is detected. Revalidate then reject or return the existing durable result. | Exact-key component hashes, state version, admission/initiative ID, hit/miss/revalidation result. |
| Resource and dialogue bounds | Request exceeds queue, bytes, context, call, retry, or two-cycle cap; timeout/outage occurs. Stop work, persist bounded degradation, preserve accepted state, pause/reconcile before replan. | Correlation ID, bound name/value, attempt/cycle count, provider failure class, recovery transition. |

The guardrails are release-blocking deterministic invariants for Phase 5. They
must remain fast, side-effect-free until one final atomic admission, and must
not log raw prompts, candidate bytes, credentials, hidden reasoning, or
machine-local paths.

## 7. Production Monitoring

There is no public production runtime in Phase 5. Monitoring is an
implementation-ready contract for local deterministic runs and the
developer-controlled subscription harness; Phase 6 owns durable external
diagnostics and Phase 7 owns live X4 operational proof.

**Trace implementation:** use the existing correlated Rust evidence tuple and
versioned fixture/benchmark manifests, not Phoenix, Langfuse, LangSmith, or a
provider SDK. A trace joins `correlation_id`, snapshot/hash, faction, request
and exact-cache identities, schema/policy/prompt hashes, provider/model
fingerprint, candidate hash/size, validator outcome, bounded usage/latency,
initiative/admission IDs, and recovery transition. Store only hashes and typed
classification codes for prompt/candidate content. Redact credentials,
machine-local paths, raw private prompts, raw provider payloads, and hidden
reasoning at the logging boundary.

| Metric | Alert / investigation rule | Sampling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Admission invariant violations | Any schema, information, safety, current-state, idempotency, or no-mutation assertion failure blocks the CI run and Phase 5 release. | 100% of deterministic cases and every harness request. |
| Bounds and degradation | Any exceeded enforced bound must produce one bounded degradation record; missing record, more than two dialogue cycles, or state change after a failure blocks the CI run. | 100% of deterministic cases and every harness request. |
| Replay and cache identity | Any same-tuple divergence or changed-component false hit blocks the CI run. | 100% of replay/cache corpus cases; every manual cache hit records revalidation. |
| Provider reliability, latency, usage, cost | Compare only within a corpus/model/configuration cohort; investigate a material shift against that cohort's recorded baseline. No numeric alert threshold exists before measurement. | Every benchmark run; retain full failing/degraded traces. |
| Strategic-quality labels | Investigate divergence between deterministic admission and human quality labels, repeated faction flattening, unsupported causal changes, or repeated churn. | Review all initial benchmark cases, then oversample failures, cache misses, preemptions, two-cycle dialogues, timeouts/recoveries, and novel model/configuration fingerprints. |

Phase 5 may block its contract release only on deterministic invariant and
corpus failures. It cannot declare strategic quality accepted, X4 behavior
observed, or the prototype playable. Phase 8 promotes subscription and human
evidence into baseline-derived internal-prototype gates after Phase 7 supplies
the required X4 evidence.
